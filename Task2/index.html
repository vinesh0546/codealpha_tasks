1. What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack in which attackers impersonate trusted individuals or organizations to trick people into revealing sensitive information or performing unsafe actions.

  • Passwords and login credentials
  • Financial information
  • Personal information
  • Access through malicious links or attachments
Key idea: Phishing attacks exploit human trust and decision-making.

2. Common Types of Phishing

Email Phishing

Fraudulent emails designed to appear to come from a trusted organization or person.

Spear Phishing

A targeted phishing attempt directed at a specific individual or organization.

Smishing

Phishing conducted through SMS or text messages.

Vishing

Phishing conducted through voice calls.

Fake Website Phishing

A fraudulent website designed to imitate a legitimate website and trick users into entering information.

3. How to Recognize Phishing Emails

  • Suspicious sender address: Check the actual email address, not only the displayed name.
  • Urgent or threatening language: Pressure to act immediately is a warning sign.
  • Unexpected links: Don't click before checking where the link leads.
  • Requests for sensitive information: Be suspicious of unexpected requests for passwords, OTPs, banking information, etc.
  • Unexpected attachments: Don't open unfamiliar attachments without verification.
  • Spelling or formatting issues: Unusual wording or inconsistent formatting can be a warning sign.
  • Unexpected context: Be cautious about messages you were not expecting.
STOP → CHECK → VERIFY → ACT

4. How to Identify Fake Websites

  • Check the URL: Look for misspelled domains, extra characters, or unexpected domains.
  • Don't rely only on appearance: Attackers can copy legitimate designs.
  • Check the connection: HTTPS encrypts communication, but HTTPS alone does not prove a site is legitimate.
  • Be cautious with unexpected login pages: Independently verify the website.
  • Watch for unusual requests: Be careful when a site unexpectedly asks for sensitive information.
Safer approach: Open your browser and enter the organization's known website yourself instead of following an unexpected login link.

5. Social Engineering Tactics

Social engineering involves deception or manipulation to influence people into revealing information or performing actions that benefit an attacker.

Urgency

“Your account will be closed in 30 minutes.”

Fear

“Your account has been compromised.”

Authority

Pretending to be a manager, bank employee, or IT administrator.

Impersonation

Pretending to be a trusted person or organization.

Curiosity

Using interesting messages or attachments to encourage a click.

Reward

Offering prizes, refunds, or other attractive benefits.

6. Best Practices to Stay Safe

  1. Think before clicking.
  2. Verify unexpected emails, messages, and calls.
  3. Check sender addresses and URLs carefully.
  4. Never share passwords or OTPs through unexpected requests.
  5. Avoid suspicious attachments.
  6. Use strong, unique passwords.
  7. Enable multi-factor authentication where available.
  8. Keep software and browsers updated.
  9. Access important services through their official websites/apps.
  10. Report suspected phishing through appropriate channels.
WHEN IN DOUBT, STOP AND VERIFY.

7. Real-World Scenarios

📧 Fake Account-Security Message

“We detected unusual activity. Verify your account immediately.”

Warning signs: urgency + unexpected link + request to log in.

Safe action: Access the service through its known official website.

📦 Fake Delivery Message

“Your package could not be delivered. Pay ₹50 to reschedule.”

Warning signs: unexpected delivery message + payment request + link.

Safe action: Check the delivery through the company's official app or website.

🏦 Fake Bank Message

“Your account will be blocked unless you update your information immediately.”

Warning signs: fear + urgency + impersonation + request for sensitive information.

Safe action: Use an official banking channel to verify the message.

8. What If You Already Clicked?

  1. Stop interacting with the suspicious page.
  2. If you entered a password, change it through the legitimate website.
  3. If that password was reused elsewhere, change it there too.
  4. If sensitive or financial information was submitted, contact the relevant organization through an official channel.
  5. Report the phishing attempt through the appropriate reporting process.
  6. Monitor the affected account for unusual activity.

10. Final Takeaways

  • Think before you click.
  • Check the sender.
  • Check the URL.
  • Never share passwords or OTPs through unexpected requests.
  • Don't trust urgency automatically.
  • Verify requests independently.
  • When in doubt, stop and verify.
Remember: Phishing awareness is about developing the habit of stopping, checking, and verifying before acting.
